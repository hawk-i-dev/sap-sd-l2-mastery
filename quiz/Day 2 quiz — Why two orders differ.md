## Day 2 quiz — Why two orders differ

Answer from memory. For multiple-choice questions, choose **one** answer.

1. What three elements make up a **sales area**?

2. Match each partner role to its function: **sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, payer**.

3. Two orders have the same sold-to customer and material but different **ship-to parties**. Name one order detail that might differ as a result.

4. In standard item category determination, which inputs are considered?
   A. Sales document type, item category group, item usage, higher-level item  
   B. Plant, storage location, shipping point  
   C. Payer, tax code, billing date

5. Which two inputs normally determine the **schedule line category**?

6. Why should you compare the **plant on each sales order item** when one order confirms stock and another confirms zero?

7. A working order uses plant **P100**; a failing order uses **P200**. Is “P200 has no stock” a **confirmed fact** or a **possible cause**? What would confirm it?

8. An item has zero confirmed quantity. Put these checks in a sensible order: **inspect the schedule lines, compare the two order items, change configuration, check availability results**. Which action should wait until you have evidence?

9. **L2 case:** Same sold-to, material, order type, and sales area. Working order: ship-to S100, plant P100, 10 confirmed. Failing order: ship-to S200, plant P200, 0 confirmed. Give your **first five investigation steps in order**.

10. In one sentence, explain why **“same customer and material”** does not prove that two orders should behave identically.

Reply as `1. ... 2. ...` and I’ll mark each answer **correct, partly correct, or incorrect**, with brief corrections and the areas to practise.
